About MinutesMap

MinutesMap turns a full game of play-by-play into one timeline so you can see who played, when momentum shifted, and which actions drove those swings.

Traditional box scores tell you what happened by the end. MinutesMap is built to show when and in what context it happened.

How to Read the Main Chart

  1. Read left to right. The chart moves through game time from Q1 to Q4 (and OT, if needed), with vertical lines marking quarter breaks.
  2. Each horizontal row is a player. The gray line shows that player's on-court stint, and event markers appear on that line at the moment they happened.
  3. Use the legend to decode markers. Colors and shapes map to events like 2PT, 3PT, FT, miss, rebound, assist, turnover, block, steal, and foul.

What the Background Means

The score-lead shape behind the player rows shows game flow at a glance. When that shape expands, the lead is growing. When it shrinks or crosses, momentum is changing hands. This lets you tie scoring runs directly to who was on the floor and what events clustered around that stretch.

Why It Can Look Dense

The chart is intentionally high-density. Instead of splitting game events into many separate pages or widgets, MinutesMap keeps timeline, player context, and event types in one place so you can scan faster once you know the visual language.

What You Can Use It For

  • Find which lineup combinations were on court during big runs.
  • Spot high-impact shifts in role players, not just star box-score totals.
  • Review late-game and clutch stretches possession by possession.
  • Compare how teams staggered minutes and substitutions.
